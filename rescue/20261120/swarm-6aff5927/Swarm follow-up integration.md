## Candidate components

- The GPU patch targets the exact N-1055 C++ preimage. It gives the compute kernel separate read-only input and write-only output buffers, preserves the 96-byte record and texture projection, and uses the barriers required by the current SSBO and texture readback consumers. Hardware timing, byte visibility and native repeatability remain open. The current ring can still retire late work and the patch leaves actor lifecycle, velocity feedback and collision for later changes.
- The C encounter scheduler accepts three independently committed physical closures, admits finite authored waves against live and placement budgets, and rotates placement attempts across available routes. A placement publishes only in its issuing tick. A later callback, closed route or old epoch rejects the ticket and the adapter retires any uncommitted prop. Final closure stops new raiders and abandons unopened wave budgets. Existing raiders drain before the Giant becomes eligible. Only actual Giant defeat can complete the encounter.
- The scheduler also allocates bounded per-actor attack tokens. Jump and drop share an airborne cap, and the same authority checks telegraph time and a fresh live, hostile, visible and in-range target generation at the hit. The trace checker tracks player incarnations separately and uses authored world-space reach limits by actor role and strike kind. The caller derives these observations from production collision, range and LOS. The token grants one hit. Retirement and expiry return the slot. The stage adapter must bound physical airborne motion before token expiry or keep the token through an actual safe landing or abort. The physical airborne count needs its own native witness. The C module and its Catch2 cases await native build and test linking.
- The executable acceptance matrix joins the authored route, actor and generation, stage epoch, and tick. Its structured replay covers tunnel closure, bounded waves, limited attacks and Giant defeat. Ten external cases require native evidence and reviewer inspection. A synthetic result, declared provider flag or supplied hash alone cannot qualify gameplay.
